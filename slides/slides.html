How many bytes does encrypted traffic classification actually need?

Observation budgets, padding effects, and an adaptive early-exit classifier for raw-byte traffic

Muhammad Talha Arshad (i232548)  ·  Zain Shahid (i232582)

The problem

Every classifier reads a prefix. Nobody measures it.

B

bytes is all a raw-byte classifier ever sees of a flow, zero-padded to a fixed length. That prefix is the observation budget.

It sets

Latency, memory, and how early you can act

A 1024-byte budget cannot decide before 1024 bytes arrive, and a middlebox holds B bytes for every concurrent flow.

0 / 12

studies from 2025 and 2026 report accuracy as a function of the budget. Budgets run from 5 to 20 packets, or 784 to 1024 bytes.

Why it matters

Any gain from a larger budget mixes two effects: more real bytes, and more padding the model can exploit as a length cue. A paper concluding “1024 bytes works best” may have measured a length classifier.

Five objectives
  • Measure the budget response, and the saturation point.
  • Test whether it is the model, not the traffic.
  • Decompose padding into representation and PAD symbol.
  • Read only what is needed, with a causality guarantee.
  • Report honestly: per-class results, and the split effect.

Two public corpora, 385 thousand sessions, 31 traffic classes, and three to eight seeds behind every number reported.

E[ρ] is the mean padding fraction of Eq. (2): max(0, k − L) / k, averaged over sessions.

Methodology

One preprocessing pass, many budgets, and a gate that halts

Parsing, sessionization and anonymisation run once. Only the dashed region repeats per budget, so the budget is the sole variable.
One store, many budgets

A single pass writes a fixed 2048-byte HDF5 session store that is sliced per budget, so sessionization, deduplication and class balance cannot drift between points on a curve. Splits are group-wise by capture file, and every point is three seeds.

The leakage gate

A halt condition, not a caveat

A(gID)  ≤  maxc πc + δ,   δ = 0.1

probe accuracy on anonymised identifier bytes ≤ majority-class rate + margin

Three probes train on byte positions alone, and the run stops unless identifier bytes are provably unlearnable. It does not warn.

Gap 4 is usually a sentence promising identifiers were removed. Here it is a precondition the pipeline enforces.

Methodology

Padding decomposed, and a causal early-exit classifier

Input modeWhat the model receives
zerobyte/255 as a scalar, padding is 0
embedlearned embedding per byte, padding still 0
masklearned embedding plus a PAD symbol
convmaskPAD symbol, convolution skips padding
length_balancedclasses resampled to equal lengths

The literature changes representation and padding symbol together, then credits the gain to padding-aware input. zero→embed gives the representation term, embed→mask the PAD-symbol term. Each is checked against the seed spread.

The exit rule

Heads at 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1024 bytes. Stop at the first head whose confidence clears τ, and charge the flow only the bytes it consumed.

exit at k = min{ k : maxc pk(c | x) ≥ τ }

This holds only if head k cannot see byte k+1

Six causal dilated convolution blocks, dilations 1 to 32, kernel 5, left-padding only, receptive field 505 bytes. Normalisation is per-position, ChannelNorm1d, never BatchNorm or GroupNorm, which average over the length axis and would let byte 900 shift the activation at byte 10.

Proved by induction, and tested: perturbing one byte reaches 505 bytes forward and 0 backward, matching the analysis exactly.

Without that guarantee the byte saving is nominal and the frontier on slide 10 is a fiction.

Same accuracy for 28% of the bytes

The gap of 0.0058 is well inside the seed spread of either, so the two are indistinguishable. On ISCX the policy reads 116 bytes, 23% of 512.

Honest in two ways

It is against the model's own heads, so no architectural advantage is smuggled in. And the encoder is causal and tested, so head k cannot have seen byte k+1.

The saving is bytes genuinely never read, not a bookkeeping claim.

We do not claim to beat these

Our ISCX figure is far below the published ones, and the table is laid out to make that clear rather than to hide it.

Three measured reasons
  • Class count. We report 11 ISCX classes where MH-Net reports 6.
  • Split policy. Worth up to +0.3782, and none of them states it.
  • Identifiers. P5's own audit shows ET-BERT falling 0.960 to 0.510 and YaTC 0.900 to 0.620 once identifier bytes are removed. Our gate removes them before the first run.

What we do claim is the budget: comparable work reads 784 to 1024 bytes where we read 144.

* quoted from the original publication under its own protocol. † macro-F1 from our runs, group-wise split.

Key findings

Five things we can now state with evidence

  • The field reads five to seven times more of each flow than the measurements support. Saturation at 512 bytes and 256, against reviewed budgets of 784 to 1024.
  • Past saturation, reading more is measurably worse, not merely unhelpful: 0.9731 down to 0.9367, at p < 10−4.
  • Saturation is a property of the traffic, not the model. A byte transformer and a 2017 CNN are not separable.
  • The padding gain is the byte embedding, not the padding symbol: +0.2408 against +0.0086.
  • An adaptive reader needs 144 bytes, not 512, with a tested causality guarantee behind the number.
The one that should change practice first

The train/test split alone is worth +0.3782 macro-F1, and none of the twelve reviewed studies states its policy.

What this does not establish
  • Our receptive field is 505 bytes, close enough to the 512-byte saturation point to be confounded on the early-exit model. The transformer control, which has no such limit, is what excludes it.
  • Gap 3 is answered as a lower bound, not a measurement.
  • Both corpora are from 2016, so 512 bytes may be a property of that cipher mix.
  • The split study uses six classes, so +0.3782 carries a class-selection bias we state alongside it.

Conclusion

Treat the budget as a measurement, not an inheritance

This project took one number the literature copies from paper to paper and made it a measured variable, on two public corpora, with a classifier that reads 28 percent of the bytes for the same accuracy and a causality guarantee behind that claim.

Future work, from the limitations
  • A corpus with more group-splittable classes, to remove the class-selection bias.
  • A larger length-balanced subset, to turn the directional padding finding into a magnitude.
  • Pre-training the byte transformer, to test whether saturation moves once the representation is learned.
  • The same sweep on contemporary traffic, to find out whether 512 bytes is a property of these 2016 captures.
